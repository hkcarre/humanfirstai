Most organisations house two distinct tribes. Truth Seekers ask uncomfortable questions, challenge assumptions, run experiments, build things, focus on outcomes, and are not afraid to fail in public. Political Players manage up, master meeting dynamics, dodge failure and excel at buzzword bingo.

We are used to seeing the Political Players prosper quickly. AI is about to change that.

## 1 · The death of cheap authority

A confident executive could go years on well-managed relationships, the right vocabulary, and an ability to stand next to anything that went well. They were all about "transformation", "strategic alignment", "operational excellence", "unlocking synergies". Pick your favourite.

Office politics worked because proof was slow. Information sat in silos. Showing a better way needed a budget. By the time the often disappointing results came in, the Political Player had already moved on to greener pastures.

AI is making all three — proof, access to information, and testing — dirt cheap.

- An analyst with a few AI tools can replace a consulting team's discovery phase in an afternoon.
- A junior can gather real customer feedback before a committee has agreed its agenda.
- Seniority no longer grants a monopoly when the information is open to everyone and hard data can answer intuition.

> AI is making substance-free leadership impossible to defend.

## 2 · High-speed nonsense versus real discovery

The counter-risk: AI is also the most potent corporate fluff generator ever created. It can churn out a forty-page deck, a business case and a risk framework in a few minutes.

So the coming divide will not be between companies using AI and companies ignoring it. It will be between two ways of using it.

- **Performative.** To manufacture the appearance of progress: more dashboards, prettier decks, automated bureaucracy.
- **Pragmatic.** To run real experiments, check real data, and ship working tools.

Both will claim large productivity gains. Only one will deliver actual business value.

## 3 · What modern leadership requires

The leaders who win this decade will be the ones secure enough to let evidence outrank authority. That means three things.

- Hiring people sharper than yourself, and giving them room.
- Rewarding finished work and informative failures over flawless presentations.
- Accepting that your job is not to win the argument, but to make sure the best idea wins.

## The bottom line

AI will not eliminate corporate politics. It raises the cost of keeping them.

The real contest is not human against machine. It is between two ideas about what organisational intelligence is for. One uses power to protect status. The other uses tools to find out what works.

> The future doesn't need more people who are brilliant at appearing right. It needs people who are brave enough to find out what's true.
