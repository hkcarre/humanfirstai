Most organisations house two distinct tribes. Truth Seekers ask uncomfortable questions, challenge assumptions, run experiments, build things and focus on outcomes, and they are unbothered to fail in public. Political Players manage up, master meeting dynamics, dodge failure and excel at buzzword-bingo.

We're used to seeing the political players prosper quickly. But AI is about to change the dynamics.

## 1. The death of cheap authority

A confident executive could go years on well-managed relationships, the right vocabulary, and an ability to stand next to anything that went well. They were all about "Transformation", "Strategic alignment", "Operational excellence", "Unlocking synergies" (pick your favourite).

Office politics worked because proof was delayed. Information sat in silos. Showing a better way needed a budget.

By the time the (often disappointing) results came in, the political player had already moved on to greener pastures.

AI is making all three (proof, information access and testing) dirt-cheap:

- An analyst with a few AI tools can run much of a consulting team's discovery phase themselves.
- A junior can gather real customer feedback before a committee even aligns on an agenda.
- Seniority no longer grants a monopoly when democratic access to information and hard data can counter intuition.

AI is making substance-free leadership much harder to defend. What it does not make cheap is judgement.

## 2. High-speed nonsense vs. real discovery

The counter-risk: AI is also the most potent corporate fluff generator ever created. It can churn out a deck of about 40 pages, a business case, and a risk framework in a few minutes.

The coming divide won't be between companies using AI and those ignoring it. It will be between:

- Performative use of AI to manufacture the appearance of progress (more dashboards, prettier decks, automated bureaucracy).
- Pragmatic use of AI to run real experiments, validate data, and ship functional tools.

Both might claim massive productivity gains. Only one will deliver actual business value.

## 3. What modern leadership requires

The leaders who win this decade will be the ones secure enough to let evidence outrank authority.

Modern leadership demands:

- Hiring people sharper than yourself and giving them autonomy.
- Rewarding finished work and informative failures over flawless presentations.
- Accepting that your job isn't to win the argument, but to ensure the best idea wins.

## The bottom line

AI won't instantly eliminate corporate politics, but it dramatically raises the cost of maintaining it.

The real AI revolution isn't human vs. machine. It's a conflict between two approaches to organisational intelligence: one that uses power to protect status, and one that uses tools to uncover what works.

The future doesn't need more people who are brilliant at appearing right. It needs people who are brave enough to find out what's true.
