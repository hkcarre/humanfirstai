# Voice

## Who is speaking

The neighbourhood teacher everyone trusts. Tea in hand. Explains hard things without making
anyone feel small, and listens twice as much as they talk. Not the founder on stage, not the
consultant, not the chatbot.

## Four principles

1. **Warm and welcoming** — write to a person, not a user base.
2. **Grounded and plain** — rent, crops, homework, market stalls. Reading age twelve.
3. **Quietly confident** — no exclamation marks, no superlatives. If a sentence needs
   "revolutionary" to land, it isn't true yet.
4. **Community-anchored** — "we" and "our neighbours", not "our users" and "our platform".

## Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Short sentences, active verbs | Stacked clauses, nominalisations |
| Describe AI by what it helps someone *do* | Describe AI as a mind, a brain, or magic |
| "Elena sorted her stall accounts in twenty minutes." | "Our model delivered superior inference." |
| Name the person, with age and city | "A user in an emerging market" |
| State the limit in the same breath | Leave limits to the small print |
| Take job and privacy fears seriously | Wave them away as misunderstanding |
| "Free." | "Free tier." |

## Banned words and phrases

Delete on sight: *AI-powered, unlock, supercharge, revolutionise, disrupt, seamless,
cutting-edge, robust, leverage, democratise, empower, game-changing, next-generation,
paradigm, ecosystem, solution, synergy.*

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| Prompt engineering | Asking clearly, then asking again |
| Hallucination | It made something up |
| Model, LLM, foundation model | The tool |
| Leveraging multi-modal heuristics for agrarian workflows | Use a phone camera to spot plant disease early |
| Democratising cognitive automation across cohorts | Free classes so everyone on our street can use this |
| Autonomous parameter tuning for pedagogical personalisation | Lessons that move at each child's pace |
| Zero-shot inference for fiscal optimisation | A five-minute tool that lays out your bills |

## Honesty rules — non-negotiable

- **Never say a tool is accurate.** Say it is often useful and sometimes confidently wrong.
- **Never imply we endorse a vendor.** We teach with whatever is free and available locally.
- **Never publish a learner's words or face without consent on file.**
- **Never state an impact number we cannot source.** If it is an estimate, write "about".
- **Never use someone's story to sell a technology.** The story belongs to them.

## Approved headlines

- AI is a tool, not a secret club.
- The future belongs to all of us.
- No computer science degree required.
- You do not need to understand algorithms to benefit from them.
- Technology works best when humans lead.
- From fear to confidence, one neighbourhood at a time.
- It is a tool. You are the one deciding.

## Naming

`Human First AI` on first mention, every time. Never `Human-First AI` — the hyphen turns
"Human" from a noun making a claim into an adjective modifying the machine.

Chapter form: `Human First AI | Nairobi` — city, not country. Formal form:
`Human First AI — Global Movement`. `HFAI` is for file names only.

## Translation

Translate meaning, not words. "Open door" carries almost everywhere; "locked lab" often does
not — use the nearest real idiom (a gate, a members-only club). Every translation is reviewed
by a native speaker before publishing, **including machine-assisted drafts**. A movement that
teaches people to check the machine cannot skip checking its own.
