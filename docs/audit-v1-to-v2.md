# Audit: v1 to v2

**Written for:** whoever owns this brand, and anyone who has to justify the changes.

A design review of the v1 package, what it got right, what was wrong, and what v2 does about
it. Every number here is reproducible with `cd tools && npm run contrast`.

---

## What v1 got right

Worth saying first, because most of it survives.

- **The strategy is sound.** "Make AI feel like an open door, not a locked lab" is a genuinely
  good brief — concrete, translatable, and it rules things out.
- **The no-hyphen argument is correct** and well reasoned.
- **Rejecting the cyan-and-purple AI aesthetic** for warm paper and ink is the right call and
  the main reason this brand looks unlike its category.
- **Atkinson Hyperlegible for body copy** is an excellent decision most brands never make.
- **The photocopy test** is the kind of constraint that comes from knowing how the work
  actually gets printed.

The problems were in execution, not direction.

---

## 1 · The mark did not read as a door

**The finding.** The v1 symbol was an arch stroke, a narrow slanted orange sliver, and a teal
dot floating in the opening. Rendered at any size it read as an archway, a monument or a
gravestone. The "door" was a thin quadrilateral that sat *inside* the aperture and merged with
the arch at small sizes. At 16px there was no door left.

Specific defects:

| | |
|---|---|
| Geometry | The leaf's top (y 24) sat above the arch's inner edge, so the two shapes collided |
| Light beam | A four-point polygon crossing the ground line and overshooting the arch on both sides — it read as a stray triangle, not light |
| The teal dot | Floating in the opening, reading as an eye or a full stop. First thing to vanish at small size, and uncomfortably close to the "no robot eyes" rule on the facing page |
| Alpha | `rgba(255,138,61,0.28)` fills — transparency breaks one-colour print, embroidery and laser etch |
| Caps | Round line caps on an architectural mark left the arch legs as bulbous stubs on the baseline |
| Centring | Visual mass left of centre; the ground line spanned 14–86 while the arch spanned 30–70 |
| Scale | No small-size variant. The claim that it stayed "crisp and distinct at 16×16" was not true of that drawing. |

**What v2 does.** A rebuilt mark: a solid arched frame standing on the ground, the door leaf
swung **outside** the frame at exactly 30° with a 2u hinge gap, a handle, and the opening
filled with Sunrise light. Three candidate directions were drawn and rendered at 256, 96, 32
and 16px in full colour, one colour and reversed before this one was chosen. A separate micro
build — heavier stroke, no handle — covers 24px and below.

---

## 2 · The published contrast figures were wrong, in the dangerous direction

**The finding.** The v1 matrix presented hand-estimated ratios as measured fact. Three of them
described failing pairs as passing.

| Pairing | v1 claimed | Actual | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| White on Community Teal `#1A8C7E` | 4.88 — "AA, approved for 14px bold or 16px regular" | **4.12** | Fails AA at both sizes |
| Community Teal on Warm Cream | 4.62 — "AA, approved for kickers and subheadings" | **3.85** | Fails AA |
| Deep Ink on Sunrise | 7.15 — "AAA" | **6.08** | AA, not AAA |
| Deep Ink on Warm Cream | 13.82 | 13.34 | Harmless, but shows the figures were not computed |

**And the one that was not in the table at all:** Sunrise `#FF8A3D` on Warm Cream is
**2.19:1** — below the 3:1 floor for graphical objects. v1 used that exact pairing for the
door leaf in the logo and for the word "AI" in the wordmark. The brand's own rule — *"do not
place the full-colour mark on backgrounds with less than 4.5:1 contrast"* — was broken by its
own primary lockup on its own primary canvas.

**What v2 does.**

- Community Teal darkened to `#187F72` — 4.55:1 on cream, 4.86:1 under white text. One colour
  that now does both jobs.
- **Sunrise Deep `#C24D00`** added so the brand can set orange type at all.
- **Sky Deep, Ink Muted and Clay** added for links, secondary text and errors — all solved
  numerically for 4.5:1, not picked by eye.
- Sunrise and Sky reclassified explicitly as **fills, never inks**.
- The mark rebuilt so the Sunrise opening is enclosed by Deep Ink on all four sides and never
  touches a light background. WCAG 1.4.11 is satisfied by the geometry, not by a rule someone
  has to remember.
- Every figure is now generated by `tools/build/color.mjs` from the hex values. They cannot
  drift from reality again.

## 3 · The wordmark contradicted the strategy it was supposed to express

**The finding.** v1's own rule: *"'Human' is set in a confident SemiBold weight and leads
visually. 'AI' is secondary, quiet, and grounded."* The implementation set "AI" in Bold, at
full size, in Sunrise — making the word that was supposed to be quietest the loudest thing on
the page, in the one colour that fails contrast on the brand's own canvas.

The wordmark was also rendered as **HTML text with CSS weights**, not as outlines. That means
the logo could not be exported as a self-contained file, would not render in Figma, Canva or
PowerPoint, and fell back to the system UI font whenever Plus Jakarta Sans failed to load —
which, on the 3G connections this brand explicitly designs for, is often.

**What v2 does.** "Human" ExtraBold 800, "First" Medium 500, "AI" Bold 700 at 0.84 cap height
— all in Deep Ink. Human leads by weight; AI is smaller and quiet. Every lockup ships as
**outlined vector paths**, generated from the licensed font files, so it renders identically
everywhere with no font dependency.

---

## 4 · The icon set did not match its own descriptions

**The finding.** Eight icons were specified with path data lifted from a general-purpose icon
library, and the descriptions did not match the drawings:

- "Open Access — an unlocked doorway with arrows indicating forward entry" was a rounded
  square with a chevron.
- "Learning Circle — a harmonious ring with people nodes sharing an open workspace" was a
  play button inside a circle.
- "Community Voice — two overlapping warm speech dialogs" was a single message square.
- "Shared Tool" was an unmodified wrench.

**What v2 does.** Twelve icons drawn for this brand on a 24px grid, each one named for the
jargon it replaces, each proven at 48, 24 and 16px. Shipped as individual SVGs, a sprite, and
a JSON index.

## 5 · Gaps between the book and what existed

| Documented | Reality |
|---|---|
| "SVG, PNG, EPS, PDF asset kit" | No asset files existed. The export modal downloaded a 12-line JSON manifest. |
| Noto Sans JP specimen for Japanese | The font was never loaded. The sample rendered in a system fallback. |
| "Pantone 7506 C" for Warm Cream | 7506 C is a mid-tan, several steps darker than `#FFF6EC`. Not a usable match. |
| "The Open Threshold framing device" | Described in prose, never drawn. |
| Swahili headline | *"Kufanya akili unde mlango wazi"* — "akili unde" is not Swahili. Should be "akili bandia". A machine-drafted line that shipped unreviewed, in a brand book that tells people to check the machine. |
| A 30-page manual | Thirty screens of prose inside a React component. Not readable by anyone without the dev server running, not diffable, not something a volunteer could open. |

## 6 · Structural problems

**No source of truth.** Colour values were repeated as literal strings in `brandData.ts`, in
`index.html`, in `index.css`, and inline across ten components. Changing the orange meant
finding every one of them.

**Guidelines that could not leave the app.** The brand book existed only as JSX. A volunteer in
Lagos who needed the logo had to clone a repository, install Node, and run a dev server.

**The typographic scale had no ratio.** Steps went 52 / 34 / 22 / 17 / 13 — ratios of 1.53,
1.55, 1.29, 1.31. That is why the headings never looked related to the body copy. v2 uses 1.26
throughout.

**13px minimum type** in a brand built around low-vision accessibility. v2 floors at 14px, and
17px for body.

## What v2 adds

| | |
|---|---|
| **Generated asset pipeline** | `tools/` produces every file in `brand/` from one definition of the mark and one palette. `npm run build`. |
| **Real logo files** | 29 lockups as outlined SVG, a PNG set, favicons, a true `.ico`, iOS and Android adaptive icons, a web manifest |
| **Design tokens** | JSON, CSS custom properties, SCSS, JS, a Tailwind preset, and an `.ase` swatch file |
| **Fonts in the repo** | Both families, OFL licences included, self-host CSS and CDN CSS |
| **28 templates** | LinkedIn, newsletter, Instagram, WhatsApp, X, slides, email signature — editable SVG plus ready-to-post PNG |
| **Photography pipeline** | Responsive web sets, arch crops, scrimmed photo cards, alt text, and a provenance gate |
| **Markdown guidelines** | Readable on GitHub, on a phone, in a pull request. Diffable. No build step. |
| **A skill** | `skill/human-first-ai-brand/` — loads the whole system into Claude Code so the team gets on-brand output without reading all of this first |

## What is still open

Honest list.

1. **Photo provenance.** The four inherited photographs have no photographer, consent or
   licence on file. They are marked not cleared. Someone has to resolve this or replace them.
2. **Pantone references are starting points**, not specifications. Pull a physical proof
   before any press run.
3. **The impact numbers** — 140,000 learners, 38 chapters, 21 languages — are carried over
   from v1 and unsourced. The voice rules forbid publishing unsourced figures. Either source
   them or stop using them.
4. **Translations beyond the samples here** have not been reviewed by native speakers.
5. **No motion guidance yet.** If the movement starts making video, that is the next chapter.

## Round three — 5 November 2026

What a native-speaker review of the Spanish, and one question about Iberian voices, turned up.

### The Spanish translation

A reviewer compared the published translation against the English and returned 27 changes:
9 grammar, 4 meaning, 10 calques, 4 style. All 27 applied, plus Spanish quotation typography.
Three were worth the review on their own:

- *la economía de la **competencia*** read as "the economics of **competition**" in a piece
  about competitors. English meant *competence*. Now *capacidad*.
- *No muevas el bote* was "don't rock the boat" translated word by word. The Spanish idiom is
  *no hagas olas*. This was the exact line flagged as uncertain when it shipped.
- The closing paragraph said who does **not** gain most and never said who does. The English
  had the same hole and the answer only existed in the page footer. Closed in both.

Two of the reviewer's suggestions were not taken as written, and the reasons are on the record:
*el orden establecido* (changed to *las cosas como están* — our rules say plain words over
institutional ones) and *en unos años* (changed to *con los años*, which keeps the English's
"over time" rather than "in a few years").

### Two bugs in our own tools, found by the review

1. **The voice checker was penalising Spanish for being Spanish.** Word-count thresholds
   calibrated on English flagged a translation with the *identical* paragraph and sentence
   count as "too dense". Now scaled per language. It is not a loosening: two genuinely long
   sentences still failed afterwards and were rewritten.
2. **The checker counted the `!` in `![image](…)` as an exclamation mark.** Fixed, along with
   counting image lines as prose paragraphs.

### The one that mattered

**Every Spanish narration we had produced was fluent, confident nonsense.** espeak-ng was
invoked with `-b=1`; it parses the value as `"=1"`, silently falls back to 8-bit, and reads
UTF-8 bytes as Latin-1. `niño` was narrated "ni a-tilde en plus or minus o". The English broke
the same day for the same reason, the moment the articles moved to curly apostrophes —
`don’t` became "doughnut tea".

Nothing caught it because nothing was wrong on paper: plausible IPA, correct duration, fluent
audio. The only test that would have caught it is listening, and the page already said nobody
had. The phonemiser now lives in one file with a check that fails the build
(`npm run phonemes:check`).

Worth sitting with: the unreviewed-translation banner was argued for on principle, and it
turned out to be literally true in a way nobody had guessed.

### Spanish from Spain

Asked for, and available two ways — `phonemeLang: es` inside Kokoro for Castilian
pronunciation, or a Piper `es_ES` voice actually recorded in Spain, run locally through
onnxruntime. Three of the latter are on the picker. Note that the article's text is Latin
American; a Castilian voice over it is a costume, not a Spain edition.

### Still open, added to the list above

6. **Nobody has listened to either narration end to end.** Both were regenerated correctly;
   neither has been heard. The Spanish page says so.
7. **Two photographs for *Give Yourself the Grace to Be Bad at It* do not exist** and the
   caption credit is a placeholder. They render as labelled empty slots and the build prints
   them. We will not fill them with a generated face.
