# 02 · Voice

## Who is speaking

The neighbourhood teacher everyone trusts. Tea in hand. Explains hard things without making
anyone feel small, and listens twice as much as they talk.

Not the founder on stage. Not the consultant. Not the chatbot.

## Four principles

**1 · Warm and welcoming.** Open arms, not open APIs. We write to a person, not a user base.

**2 · Grounded and plain.** We talk about rent, crops, homework, market stalls, clinic queues.
Never "synergistic paradigms". Aim at reading age twelve.

**3 · Quietly confident.** No exclamation marks. No "revolutionary". No "game-changing". If a
sentence needs a superlative to land, it is not true yet.

**4 · Community-anchored.** "We" and "our neighbours", not "our users" and "our platform".

## Do and don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Short sentences. Active verbs. | Stacked clauses and nominalisations. |
| Describe AI by what it helps someone *do*. | Describe AI as a mind, a brain, or magic. |
| "Elena sorted her stall accounts in twenty minutes." | "Our model delivered superior inference." |
| Name the person. Give their age and city. | "A user in an emerging market." |
| Say what it cannot do in the same breath. | Leave the limits to the small print. |
| Take job and privacy fears seriously. | Wave them away as misunderstanding. |
| "Free." | "Free tier." |

## The jargon list

The left column is banned in anything public.

| Instead of | Say |
|---|---|
| Leveraging multi-modal transformer heuristics for agrarian workflows | Use a phone camera to spot plant disease early |
| Democratising cognitive automation across demographic cohorts | Free classes so everyone on our street can use this |
| Generative conversational copilot for inventory management | A simple chat tool that helps you count your stock |
| Algorithmic decision support reducing clinical triage friction | A plain guide that helps a health worker organise checkups |
| Autonomous parameter tuning for pedagogical personalisation | Lessons that move at each child's pace |
| Zero-shot inference pipelines for fiscal optimisation | A five-minute tool that lays out your bills |
| Prompt engineering | Asking clearly, then asking again |
| Hallucination | It made something up |
| Model, LLM, foundation model | The tool |
| AI-powered | *(delete the phrase and say what it does)* |
| Unlock, supercharge, revolutionise, disrupt, seamless, cutting-edge, robust | *(delete)* |

## Honesty rules

Not style preferences. Breaking these damages the movement.

- **Never say a tool is accurate.** Say it is often useful and sometimes confidently wrong.
- **Never imply we endorse a vendor.** We teach with whatever is free and available locally.
- **Never publish a learner's words or face without consent on file.**
- **Never state an impact number we cannot source.** If it is an estimate, write "about".
- **Never use someone's story to sell a technology.** The story belongs to them.

## Headlines that work

Use these, or write in their shape.

- AI is a tool, not a secret club.
- The future belongs to all of us.
- No computer science degree required.
- You do not need to understand algorithms to benefit from them.
- Technology works best when humans lead.
- From fear to confidence, one neighbourhood at a time.
- It is a tool. You are the one deciding.

## The voice applied to a real piece

[The Great Corporate Unmasking](https://hkcarre.github.io/humanfirstai/articles/the-great-corporate-unmasking.html)
came to us written for LinkedIn. The argument was good and survived intact. The voice work
was almost entirely about **shape**, which is the part people miss.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Words | 1,482 | 1,420 |
| Sentences | 149 | 134 |
| Average sentence | 9.9 words | 10.6 words |
| **Paragraphs** | **136** | **44** |
| **Average paragraph** | **10.9 words** | **32.3 words** |
| Banned terms in our own voice | 9 | 0 |

### The sentences were never the problem

At 9.9 words a sentence, the original already read plainly. What made it off-brand was that
**almost every sentence was its own paragraph.**

That staccato is a house style, and it is not ours. It is the same register as the
hook-and-thread opener and the "Agree? 👇" sign-off — writing that performs for an algorithm
rather than talking to a person. It makes a reader feel handled.

A paragraph is a unit of thought. When each one holds three or four sentences, the reader can
see where an idea starts and stops. When each one holds a single line, every sentence is
shouted at the same volume and nothing is load-bearing.

**Keep the short sentences. Join them into paragraphs.**

### Six edits that show the difference

| Before | After | Why |
|---|---|---|
| AI is beginning to disrupt all three. | AI is making all three cheap. | "Disrupt" is on the list. The replacement is also more precise — it says *how*. |
| …an almost supernatural ability to associate themselves with successful initiatives. | …a gift for standing next to things that went well. | Same joke, plainer words, funnier. |
| Welcome to Corporate Theatre 2.0. | *(cut)* | A punchline that congratulates the writer. The paragraph lands harder without it. |
| AI can hallucinate. | They make things up. | "Hallucinate" dresses up a plain fact. We never let a technical word hide a limitation. |
| Better healthcare. More accessible education. Scientific breakthroughs. Environmental sustainability. | Better care. Schools that reach further. A trader who can check her own stock figures. A farmer who gets a frost warning in time. | Four abstractions became things you can picture. Two of them have a person in them. |
| **AI Is Coming for the Politicians** *(subtitle)* | **When proof gets cheap, talk gets expensive** | "Coming for" is a threat. We are quietly confident, not combative — and the replacement states the actual argument. |

### What we deliberately left alone

- **The eight-section structure**, and the section titles' sense of humour.
- **The dialogue scene.** It is the best thing in the piece: concrete, plain, no adjectives.
- **"Unlocking synergies", "Transformation", "Strategic alignment"** — these stay, in quotation
  marks, in a list of things being mocked. Naming jargon in order to puncture it is exactly what
  the [jargon list](#the-jargon-list) is for. The rule is that we do not use these words
  *in our own voice*, not that we pretend nobody says them.
- **The author's best lines**, including "a brilliant idea without execution is simply a more
  entertaining form of procrastination" and the closing pair about appearing right versus
  finding out what's true.

### One thing we added

The brand requires that we state a limit in the same breath as a claim. The original noted that
AI can be wrong; the rewrite makes it unmissable, because a piece arguing for evidence cannot
be vague about its own:

> These tools also get things wrong with great confidence. They make things up. They will
> happily reinforce a flawed assumption and turn selective evidence into a persuasive case.
> Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

### Checking your own draft

```bash
# from the repo root — counts paragraphs, sentence length and banned terms
node skill/human-first-ai-brand/scripts/voice-check.mjs your-piece.md
```

Aim for **15–35 words a paragraph** and **zero banned terms outside quotation marks**. If your
average paragraph is under 12 words, you are performing rather than talking.

## Translation

Translate the **meaning**, not the words. "Open door" carries almost everywhere. "Locked lab"
often does not — in many languages the nearest idiom is a gate, a members-only club, or a
locked gate at the edge of a field. Use whichever one lands.

Every translation is reviewed by a native speaker from that chapter before it is published,
including machine-assisted drafts. Especially machine-assisted drafts. A movement that teaches
people to check the machine cannot skip checking its own.

### Machine-assisted translation

A draft from a machine is a draft. It is published only with an unmissable notice saying it
has not been reviewed, and it stays that way until a native speaker from that chapter has read
it and said so. The notice is not a disclaimer to tuck in a footer — it sits above the article,
in the reader's language, in Sunrise.

Worked example, currently awaiting review:
[La gran caída de las máscaras](https://hkcarre.github.io/humanfirstai/articles/the-great-corporate-unmasking.es.html).

## The voice checker and other languages

`voice-check.mjs` counts words. Word counts do not travel between languages: the same
sentence takes more words in Spanish or French than in English, so English thresholds mark a
faithful translation as "too dense" for doing its job.

The checker reads `lang` from the article's frontmatter and scales the **word** targets by the
usual localisation expansion — Spanish, Portuguese and French ×1.2, Italian ×1.15, German and
Dutch ×1.1. It never scales the **shape**: paragraph and sentence counts are still expected to
match the original, and those are what we actually care about. The Spanish translation of *The
Great Corporate Unmasking* has exactly the same 44 paragraphs and 136 sentences as the
English.

This is a measurement fix, not a relaxation. If a translation is genuinely dense it still
fails — the Spanish did, on two sentences, after the thresholds were corrected, and both were
rewritten.
